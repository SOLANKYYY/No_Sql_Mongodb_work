=== Step 4: Analysis - query performance vs write throughput ===
  Inserting 10,000 documents took 0.059s with no indexes and 0.402s with 5 indexes (+581.2% write time; 169,545 vs 24,888 docs/sec), because every insert must also update all 5 index B-trees. In return, indexed queries read only matching entries instead of scanning the whole collection. After dropping to the single idx_user_id index, 4 of 5 queries fell back to COLLSCAN (Q2 email lookup, Q3 city equality, Q4 age range 30-31, Q5 score >= 99.5) and 4 of 5 became at least 1.5x slower (Q2 email lookup, Q3 city equality, Q4 age range 30-31, Q5 score >= 99.5). Trade-off: every extra index buys read speed for the queries it serves but costs write throughput and storage on every insert, so keep only the indexes that serve frequent, selective queries. Read-heavy workloads justify more indexes; write-heavy workloads (logs, IoT) should keep them minimal.
